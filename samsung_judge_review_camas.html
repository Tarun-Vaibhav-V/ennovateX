Gap 1 — fatal if not fixed Your "before vs after" is simulated on training data
You plan to "replay real traces with and without CAM" — but these are the same traces you used to design your controller thresholds. Your EVICT threshold was chosen because it works on these specific traces. Showing improvement on your own training data is circular. A judge who knows ML will immediately spot this and it will invalidate your entire KPI table.
Fix right now: Record 2 new Perfetto traces TONIGHT — different apps, different time of day. Lock them in a folder called /test_traces/. Never let your threshold tuning or LSTM training touch these. All final KPI numbers come from these traces only.
Gap 2 — kills demo score No physical device moment in the demo
Your demo is: Python dashboard → simulated replay → simulated KPI numbers. There is no phone on the table. This is the "PowerPoint architecture" problem — it looks great until a judge says "can you show me on the actual device?" and the answer is no.
Fix: Add one ADB command to your demo: adb shell am force-stop com.samsung.android.gallery && adb shell am start -W com.samsung.android.gallery. Run it without CAMAS (baseline). Then run it after your ADB-based preload command fires. Show real milliseconds. Even if the improvement is only 15%, it's real and judges respect real.
Gap 3 — weakens ML credibility Prediction accuracy is a claim, not a measurement
The plan targets ≥75% accuracy but hasn't trained the LSTM yet. The Markov chain is not built. These are projections. Judges will ask: "what is your actual measured accuracy on your test data?" If you can't answer this with a number and a confusion matrix, KPI 5 is unproven.
Fix: Build the Markov chain first (1–2 hours). Evaluate it on the held-out test trace immediately. Even 55% accuracy is a real number. It beats the random baseline (5%) by 11×. Say that. Then the LSTM improvement on top of that is the bonus.
Gap 4 — weakens score Memory utilization efficiency metric is self-defined and unchallengeable
KPI 7 uses "useful_memory = memory used by apps launched within 30s" — but you choose the 30-second window. A judge can ask: "why 30 seconds? At 60 seconds your efficiency score drops by half." Self-defined metrics invite this challenge.
Fix: Anchor to an industry standard. Use working set size (WSS) — the set of pages accessed in the last N seconds as defined by Linux kernel documentation. Or use PSI reduction as your efficiency proxy — it's a kernel-standard measure that doesn't require you to define "useful."
